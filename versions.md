# Versions — Variant A: home Wi-Fi / router (password)

Concept: the router's password rule applied to the WRONG target — its own owner.
Skeleton: named artifact (Wi-Fi password) + security rule (impossible to guess)
+ inversion (the person locked out is me) + twist lands on the last word.

## v1 (151 chars)
I changed the Wi-Fi password to something impossible to guess, and now the only person who can't get online in this house is me.
Why it changed: "who can't get online" is wordy; "locked out of my own house" is a stronger, more visual inversion.

## v2 (118 chars)
I changed the Wi-Fi password to something nobody could guess, and now the only person locked out of my own house is me.
Why it changed: tighter ("nobody could guess" reads more natural than "impossible to guess"); "locked out of my own house" sharpens the inversion — but "changed the" vs "set" still to test.

## v3 (116 chars)
I set the Wi-Fi password to something nobody could ever guess, and now the only person locked out of this house is me.
Why it changed: "set" is one syllable shorter and crisper; "ever" adds rhythm — but "this house" loses the "my own" sting, and "ever" is a spare word.

## v4 (115 chars)
Our Wi-Fi password is something nobody could ever guess, which is why the only person locked out of this house is me.
Why it changed: passive opening ("Our Wi-Fi password is...") is flatter than the active "I changed/set" — the actor doing it to himself is the joke. Reverting to active voice next.

## v5 (115 chars)
I set our Wi-Fi password to something nobody could guess, and now the only person locked out of my own house is me.
Why it changed: "set our" is tight, but "our" softens the self-inflicted sting — the joke is that *I* did this to *myself*, so "the" keeps it personal.

## v6 (119 chars)
I changed our Wi-Fi password to something nobody could guess, and now the only person locked out of my own house is me.
Why it changed: "changed" is the right verb (an action I took), but "our" again dilutes it — the owner should be the one locked out, not the household.

## v7 (104 chars)
I made the Wi-Fi password impossible to guess, and now the only person locked out of my own house is me.
Why it changed: shortest so far and punchy, but "made ... impossible to guess" is slightly stiff vs the natural "changed ... to something nobody could guess". One more pass for the most natural spoken rhythm.

## v8 — FINAL (119 chars, 119 bytes)
I changed the Wi-Fi password to something nobody could guess, and now the only person locked out of my own house is me.
Why it wins: active self-inflicted verb ("I changed"), everyday scene everyone knows, inversion (the password's creator is the one locked out), twist lands on the very last word ("me"), one sentence, natural rhythm aloud, no spare words, nothing a judge has heard verbatim before.
